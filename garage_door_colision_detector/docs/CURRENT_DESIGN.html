Wired through-beam design

5 September 2026 · Current architecture decision · Supersedes the retroreflective and 600 mm enclosure proposals

Power review — 6 September

Retain the single Murata 5 V / 1.5 A buck and 470 µF bulk capacitors already in the power schematic. Generator package requirements for C1/C5 were corrected; native migration and physical verification remain pending. Read the power-stage review and reviewed parts list.

Build direction

  • One ESP32-DevKitC V4 / ESP32-DEVKITC-32E in the controller enclosure.
  • Three matched, internally modulated industrial through-beam sensor pairs at nominal 100 mm optical-axis centres; electronics expandable to four inputs.
  • Controller side: receivers, protected input circuits, ESP32 and status indicators. Opposite side: powered emitters and power conditioning; no second ESP32.
  • Latest power decision: independent certified regulated 12 V DC adapters, one per side. Each uses a 5.5 × 2.1 mm barrel plug, centre-positive. Use panel-mounted mating sockets wired to the existing two-position power terminals. Controller side: protected 12 V sensor rail plus 12-to-5 V buck for the ESP32. Emitter side: protected 12 V distribution. No USB-C power-input circuitry or boost converter is required.
  • No photovoltaic charger, battery, LCD shutter or modulated retroreflector in this implementation. No mirror is required.
  • Exact sensor model and whether LED or laser remain pending actual optical span, detection-boundary requirements and supplier verification. Do not order the former EX-L291 retroreflective head for this architecture.

Receiver output convention: NPN Light-ON, positively asserted only with a received beam. Pulse modulation is an optical rejection feature, not a claim of unique per-beam coding. Check adjacent-beam cross-talk and manufacturer installation restrictions.

12 V barrel input and mains boundary

Connector specification: 5.5 mm outer × 2.1 mm inner mating plug, centre-positive, 12 V DC only. Prefer a panel-mounted female jack with insulated rear contacts and cable strain relief. Wire its centre contact to the existing +12 V terminal and its sleeve to GND. If the selected jack includes a switched contact, identify it from its datasheet and leave it insulated and unused; do not infer pin numbers from appearance. Select one exact jack before final mounting-hole and 3D-model work.

The barrel socket and screw terminal are alternative entry methods for one supply per box; never connect two adapters to them simultaneously. A barrel connector does not enforce voltage or polarity. Keep fuse, reverse-polarity and transient protection downstream of either entry. Confirm adapter regulation, sensor minimum voltage after protection losses, and converter current requirements. Do not substitute a 5.5 × 2.5 mm plug or an AC-output adapter.

Both custom boards remain low-voltage only. Keep adapters intact and preferably outside the sensor boxes, connected to properly installed outlets. Enclosing an adapter requires assessment of permitted thermal conditions, mains segregation, guarding, strain relief and access by a qualified electrician. No dismantled chargers or bare mains conversion boards.

Keep the two isolated adapter outputs separate; the optical link needs no cross-garage ground wire. Retain the controller's external-5 V disconnect or a verified power multiplexer to prevent backfeeding its programming USB port. Never apply 12 V to the DevKit's 5 V or 3.3 V pins.

Check the exact panel jack, plug insertion/removal space, lead bends and adapter placement before releasing the enclosure. Oscilloscope use remains optional for basic commissioning with industrial sensors. The current native schematic already has a two-pin 12 V terminal and buck stage; the external barrel-jack harness and emitter-side distribution are not yet drawn in the native project.

Mounting envelope

Start with 100 × 100 × 300 mm outer width × depth × height for three beams. Three centres at 100 mm pitch span 200 mm. Illustrative centres are 50, 150 and 250 mm above the outer enclosure bottom, leaving nominal 50 mm end allowances. Those allowances must contain head and bracket extents, wall thickness, fasteners and cable clearance; they are not guaranteed usable space.

Increase height only if the assembled parts require it. Four beams at the same pitch span 300 mm and would need a taller enclosure, approximately 400 mm with the same nominal end allowances.

Keep the PCB short and separate from a rigid optical mounting rail. Individual brackets provide pitch/yaw adjustment and locking. Rectangular resistor symbols with values inside remain the requested schematic style. No PCB dimensions or drilling pattern are released.

One build, then commissioning

The installed first build is intended to remain the final hardware, not a disposable experiment. Nevertheless it cannot be accepted without testing the actual installation: check clear/blocked transitions with the car at relevant angles; interrupt each beam; disconnect each sensor and emitter supply; power cycle; check adjacent-beam cross-talk; repeat in relevant lighting conditions. Confirm the whole moving-door envelope and unmonitored gaps between beam heights.

Missing signal, uncertain input or startup must never produce green. All enabled beams must remain clear for 500 ms before green. Existing single-output inputs cannot detect every defect, including some stuck-on outputs and signal shorts to ground. Advisory only; no door actuation or manufacturer safety-sensor replacement.

Implementation status

This decision updates documentation only. Native schematics, PCB, firmware, purchasing BOM and complete 3D assembly have not yet been migrated to this architecture. Older procurement and wiring documents are historical, not build instructions. The native schematic repair status describes their last verified state.

The next selection input is the actual emitter-to-receiver optical span. Then verify exact head dimensions, pins/wires, voltage range, modulation/cross-talk behaviour and terminal requirements before changing the native schematic.
