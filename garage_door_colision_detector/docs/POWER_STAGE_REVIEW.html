One buck regulator, with transient support

6 September 2026 · Power selection review, not a fabrication release

Retained circuit

12 V barrel socket harness → J1 → F1 protection → D6 reverse-polarity diode → protected sensor supply and A2 buck. A2 feeds 5V_LOGIC; JP1 connects it to the DevKit's 5 V input. The development board supplies its own 3.3 V rail. No parallel regulator outputs and no additional 3.3 V converter.

A2 remains Murata OKI-78SR-5/1.5-W36-C. Its rated 1.5 A output provides margin above our provisional 1 A total 5 V rail design budget; temperature, adapter, fuse and wiring limits still apply. The emitter side needs only protected 12 V distribution, not another buck or ESP32.

C1 remains 470 µF / 25 V at the protected input. C5 remains 470 µF / 10 V on 5V_LOGIC near the DevKit power entry. Retain the development board's local decoupling. Select any additional ceramics from the regulator and DevKit requirements, not by indiscriminately increasing bulk capacitance.

Capacitor limits and placement

The Murata 5 V data table specifies different capacitive-load limits for different ESR conditions: 300 µF in the very-low-ESR 1–10 mΩ region and 3300 µF for its stated 0.01 Ω condition. Do not replace the intended electrolytic with an arbitrary ultra-low-ESR polymer bank. Validate total connected capacitance, startup and load-step behaviour.

Panasonic specifies a maximum impedance of 56 mΩ at 100 kHz for C5; this is not a guaranteed minimum ESR. Consequently this review does not claim full loop-stability qualification of C5 from capacitance alone. If that qualification is unavailable, revise the capacitor network before releasing the design.

Illustrative calculation: a 470 µF capacitor supplies an additional 200 mA for 0.47 ms with 0.2 V ideal droop. This excludes ESR, wiring and regulation effects, and is not a measured ESP32 transient. Short power/ground connections matter.

References: Murata datasheet, 5 V specifications; Panasonic C5.

Mechanical correction

C5 is 8 mm diameter × 11.5 mm long, 3.5 mm lead pitch; the generator's generic 10 mm intended-package entry was wrong. Its source now requests CAP_RADIAL_D8_P3.5. C1 is 10 mm diameter × 12.5 mm long with 5 mm lead pitch and now requests CAP_RADIAL_D10_P5. These names specify required footprints; they do not create or verify physical library footprints.

The native sheet has not been rebuilt. Do not rerun the full rebuild script over manual edits without a fresh backup and controlled review. The existing sheet was copied to History/power-review-20260906 before UI inspection.

Electrical checks before relying on the installation

  • Check 12 V polarity and rail voltage under load, including the drop across F1/D6. Sensor minimum voltage must be met at its cable end.
  • Check 5 V and 3.3 V with the DevKit and all indicators operating, then exercise Wi-Fi reconnect/transmit while checking for resets. A multimeter cannot certify brief droop; use a scope if behaviour is unstable or quantitative transient verification is needed.
  • Confirm JP1 is removed before programming USB is attached. Do not connect two independent supplies to the same input or parallel the buck outputs.
  • Verify the input protection, fuse coordination and enclosure temperature with the final sensor load. Murata's reference fuse is not automatically equivalent to the draft's 1.1 A PTC; coordination remains to be checked.
  • Check blockage and power-loss response on every optical channel. Existing static input circuitry does not diagnose every stuck-on or shorted-signal fault.
